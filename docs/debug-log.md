# Debug log

Every bug and investigation in the project — scene, environment, expert,
recording, evaluation and training — oldest first. Each entry: **symptom → how it was found → cause → fix
→ what guards it now.** "Test" names refer to `tests/test_scene.py`,
`tests/test_env.py` and `tests/test_task_<task>.py`.

The working method, in short: measure instead of eyeballing a render; when
something fails, narrow it down with one targeted measurement per hypothesis;
check whether a check itself measures the right thing; test parts in
isolation before combining them.

## Scene

### 1. Table legs floating 1.5 cm above the floor
- **Symptom:** none visible — the first render looked fine.
- **Found by:** computing each table geom's z-range after building step 1.
- **Cause:** leg half-length 0.135 m centred at −0.15 m → legs spanned
  z = 0.015 … 0.285.
- **Fix:** half-length 0.1425 m at −0.1575 m → z = 0 … 0.285.
- **Guard:** none automated (fixed geometry).

### 2. Arm bases sunk 2.4 mm into the tabletop
- **Found by:** measuring the lowest vertex of each base mesh after attaching
  the arms: z = 0.3126 vs the surface at 0.315.
- **Cause:** the SO-101 base mesh extends 2.4 mm below its body origin.
- **Fix:** mount frames at z = 0.3174.
- **Guard:** `test_arm_bases_rest_on_the_tabletop`. A contact-based test would
  not catch this: base and table are both welded to the world, and MuJoCo
  skips contacts between two such bodies — found when a reintroduced copy of
  this bug passed every other test.

### 3. physicalai's home pose drove the grippers into the table
- **Found by:** settling the scene and measuring joint error: 0.68 rad on
  `wrist_flex` with bases on the tabletop (0.27 rad in a later run).
- **Cause:** that pose was tuned for bases at z = 0.40, 8.5 cm higher.
- **Fix:** search a new home pose (`scripts/find_home_pose.py`): sampled IK
  + local refinement, checked for contacts and a 3 s hold.
- **Guard:** `test_arms_hold_without_contacts`.

### 4. "Contacts" at a pose that had none
- **Symptom:** the home-pose search reported contacts for its best pose.
- **Found by:** listing the contact pairs: arm A touching arm B.
- **Cause:** the check moved arm A but left arm B at all-zeros (upright), where
  the two arms intersect. A bug in the check, not the pose.
- **Fix:** set both arms before checking.

### 5. A new joint silently shifted every keyframe
- **Found by:** printing the joint order after adding the bins.
- **Cause:** joints are ordered by their body's place in `<worldbody>`. The
  bins are children of the table, which precedes the arms, so the bin joints
  went to the *front* of `qpos`. The old 12-value `home` key would have fed
  bin values into arm joints without any warning.
- **Fix:** rewrite the keys with bin values first; later, generate keys with
  `find_home_pose.py` instead of typing them.
- **Guard:** `test_keyframe_ctrl_matches_arm_qpos`, `test_bins_open_key`.

### 6. The can landed on the floor at the world origin
- **Symptom:** after settling, the can was at (0, 0, 0.05).
- **Found by:** comparing `qpos0` (spawn pose, correct) with the compiled
  keyframe's values for the can (0, 0, 0).
- **Cause:** the key listed fewer values than `nq`. MuJoCo pads a short key,
  and for a free joint the padding is the world origin — not the spawn pose,
  despite the docs' "default configuration" wording.
- **Fix:** list every free object in every key (generated by script).
- **Guard:** since items are no longer in the scene file (design-decisions
  D17), keys hold only bins and arms: `test_keyframes_hold_only_bins_and_arms`.

### 7. A keyframe one value too long is a load error
- **Symptom:** `invalid qpos size, expected 154, got 155` after removing the
  middle bin.
- **Note:** the opposite of #6 — too short is silent, too long fails loudly.

### 8. Wrist cameras showed clipped slivers of the jaws
- **Symptom:** yellow rod-like shapes in both wrist views after adding the
  20 items.
- **Found by:** (1) a segmentation render — those pixels belonged only to the
  gripper's own visual meshes; (2) gripper joints unchanged; (3) computing the
  near clip plane: `znear × extent` = 0.01 × 5.0 m = 5 cm, while the nearest
  jaw vertex is 3.5 cm from the wrist camera.
- **Cause:** items parked 2.5 m away grew the model extent from ~1 m to 5 m,
  and the near plane scales with it.
- **Fix:** `<visual><map znear="0.002"/>` → 1 cm.
- **Guard:** `test_near_plane_closer_than_the_jaws`.

### 9. No arm could reach behind a bin handle
- **Found by:** IK at the hook point of a closed bin, for every arm/bin pair
  and approach direction: all failed; reachable once the bin was 4 cm out.
- **Cause:** with a 2.5 cm handle, the gap behind it was 1 cm in front of the
  table edge, 4 cm below the top — the gripper hits the table edge.
- **Fix:** sweep of standoff distances (1–6 cm): ≥ 4 cm works for all arms →
  handles at 5 cm.
- **Guard:** `test_arm_opens_its_bin` (fails on a reintroduced 2.5 cm handle).

## Reach analysis — mistakes in the checks themselves

### 10. "Each arm only reaches its own bin" — wrong
- **Cause:** the reach map sampled only points *below* the table edge. Items
  go into an open bin from *above*, and there both arms reach every bin.
- **Found by:** a later test of the drop point above each open bin.

### 11. "Arm A can't reach over the metal bin" — first from an over-strict check
- **Cause:** the IK demanded the gripper point straight down; at that range
  the arm is too short for any vertical pose. Dropping only needs the can's
  centre over the bin.
- **Fix:** position-only IK for drops, wrist angle free.

### 12. "Arm A reaches the metal bin only upside down" — wrong
- **Symptom:** at bins x = ±0.20 the reported pose had the gripper at 172°.
- **Found by:** a reintroduced "bins back at ±0.20" passed the drop test even
  with a 75° tilt limit; 200 IK restarts then found 44° solutions.
- **Cause:** the check kept the solution with the smallest position error;
  an upside-down pose tied with upright ones at 0 mm.
- **Consequence:** the user moved the bins partly on this wrong result. The
  real blocker at ±0.20 was the handle (#9). ±0.10 is still easier (15°).
- **Guard:** `test_arm_can_drop_into_open_bin` with `max_tilt=75`.

## Expert

### 13. A top-down grasp of the can is impossible
- **Symptom:** IK failed for the pre-grasp pose 6 cm above the can.
- **Found by:** mapping the vertical-gripper reach band by height: the
  fingertips reach only up to z ≈ 0.40; the can's top is 0.417. Then a map of
  approach directions (elevation 0–90°, 8 headings): only straight down works.
- **Fix:** side slide-in grasp — lower the open, vertical gripper beside the
  can and slide it sideways so the can passes between the thin jaws.

### 14. Wrong retreat point after IK restarts
- **Found by:** reading the code: after random restarts, `ArmIK.d` holds the
  *last attempt*, not the returned solution.
- **Fix:** compute points from the returned joint angles (`_point`).

### 15. 4/10 episodes failed: the can ended up lying on the table
- **Found by:** per-phase log (grasp and lift fine) → planned vs actual can
  pose at the drop (plan correct; arm A 0.71 rad off its target) → contact list
  during the carry: arm A's gripper vs arm B's wrist camera mount.
- **Cause:** arm B returned to home, which is in arm A's drop path.
- **Fix:** arm B parks beside its own base (`PARK_B`).

### 16. 1/40 episodes: the can was thrown off the table
- **Symptom:** the can ended on the floor 2.5 m away.
- **Found by:** tracing back from the end state: off the far edge at 10.5 s
  → lying on its side already at 3 s → first arm contact at 0.38 s, the
  moving jaw, while travelling to the first waypoint.
- **Cause:** the gripper opened while swinging past the can; at home the
  jaws hang just above the can's top.
- **Fix:** lift over the can closed, open only beside it.
- **Guard:** `test_expert_succeeds[t0_can_to_metal_bin-25]`; smoke test flags "can moved >1 cm
  before the grasp".

### 17. The bin opened only to ~0.13 m
- **Cause:** the fingertip lags the pull target, then pushed the bin back while
  leaving the gap.
- **Fix:** pull target 0.20 m; leave the gap upward and slightly outward.
  Now ≈ 0.16 m — enough for the drop.

## Performance

### 18. Pouring with particles: 55–90 % spilled, then too slow
- **Found by:** mid-pour render (spheres near the start, not the bin) →
  counting spheres per phase (lost during the carry) → tracking where each
  sphere left the bowl (floor height, at the floor–wall seam).
- **Causes:** the bowl was a *mocap* body (teleported each step, invisible to
  the contact solver as motion); smoothing the motion made it worse, which
  ruled out acceleration. Separately, the default solver took 130 ms/step
  with 200 stacked spheres.
- **Fixes:** a free bowl pulled by a weld (98 % in the bin);
  `solver="CG" jacobian="sparse"` (~30× faster).

### 19. Image rendering ≈ 0.15 s per step
- **Found by:** timing physics vs rendering separately: physics 1.8 ms per
  control step, rendering 3 cameras at 224² 143 ms.
- **Breakdown (shadows kept on):** both lights cast shadows (one extra
  full-scene pass each); 311k triangles of parked items; and the **arms'
  visual meshes: 798k triangles**, 82 % of everything drawn. Shadow-map size
  (512–4096) and anti-aliasing made no difference.
- **Action:** design-decisions D17–D20.

## Evaluation (phase 3 preparation)

### 26. The last frame of an evaluation video showed the starting scene
- **Symptom:** in the first evaluation video the final frame jumped back to
  the start (can on the table, bins closed) instead of the can in the bin;
  success was still reported correctly.
- **How found:** looking at first, middle and last frames of the video.
- **Cause:** LeRobot's default `EnvConfig.create_envs` builds the vector env
  with `AutoresetMode.SAME_STEP`: a sub-env whose episode ends is reset
  within that same `step()`, so rendering after the step shows the new
  episode (success is passed on in `final_info`).
- **Fix:** our config's `create_envs` wraps each env in LeRobot's
  `FreezeAfterEpisodeEnd` (lerobot/envs/utils.py), which replays the final
  step instead of resetting until a new rollout starts; finished sub-envs also
  stop simulating while the rest of a batch runs.
- **Guard:** none automatic; check a video's last frame after changing env
  creation.

### 27. Identity action postprocessor rejected the expert's action
- **Symptom:** `ValueError: EnvTransition must be a dictionary. Got Tensor`
  in `rollout()` at `postprocessor(action)`.
- **Cause:** `PolicyProcessorPipeline(steps=[])` converts its input as an
  observation batch (a dict); LeRobot builds action postprocessors with
  `to_transition=policy_action_to_transition` and
  `to_output=transition_to_policy_action` (lerobot/processor/factory.py).
- **Fix:** the expert's identity postprocessor uses those converters.

### 28. Expert fails on 2 of 50 evaluation seeds: can knocked over in the grasp
- **Symptom:** expert evaluation on seeds 1000–1049 (D27): 48/50 (96%);
  seeds 1004 and 1009 run to the 25 s limit.
- **How found:** `scripts/evaluate_expert.py`, then `smoke_test.py --seeds
  1004 1009 --video-all` for the per-phase log and video.
- **What happens:** while the jaws slide around the can and close
  (t ≈ 2.8–3.8 s) the can tilts (centre z 0.366 → 0.375); on the lift it
  falls on its side (z 0.349) and stays on the table; the rest of the plan
  runs with an empty gripper. The smoke test flags it ("moved > 1 cm before
  the grasp").
- **Where:** both cans start in the corner of `CAN_PATCH` nearest arm A's
  base: (x, y) = (−0.160, −0.093) and (−0.154, −0.084). Other evaluation
  seeds with x < −0.15 but y ≥ −0.072 succeed. Training seeds 0–49 reach
  that corner only once (seed 3, (−0.151, −0.088), succeeded), so the
  recorded demonstrations hardly cover it.
- **How the cause was found:** logged every contact with the can and the
  planned waypoints, and compared with seed 1027 (can at almost the same
  spot, success). First contact in the failures: the wrist camera housing
  (`left_camera_box2`) while lowering beside the can, before the jaws touch.
- **Cause 1 (1004, 1009):** the expert slides in along the gripper's y axis
  and tries the side toward arm A's base first. Near the base that start
  point (8.5 cm from the can's axis) is just out of reach (11.7 mm / 3.1 mm
  error, limit 2 mm), so it used the other side, where the camera housing
  leads and hits the can.
- **Cause 2 (184, found when recording seeds 0–199):** same side as usual,
  but the IK restarts returned the other arm configuration for the waypoints
  beside the can — wrist rolled ~half a turn (0.73 vs −2.74 rad), gripper
  facing the other way, camera housing 1.9 cm from the can's axis (12 cm
  normally). The restarts keep the solution nearest in joint angles, which
  does not check which way the gripper faces.
- **Fix (user decided to fix the grasp, 2026-10-06), `sim/experts.py`:**
  try shorter slide starts on the base side (8.5, 7.5, 6.5 cm, `SLIDES`)
  before the other side; and the waypoints before the grasp must keep the
  grasp's jaw direction (`_solve(..., jaw_axis=...)`, within 20°).
- **Result:** evaluation seeds 1000–1049: 50/50; training seeds 0–199:
  200/200, none with the can moved before the grasp.
- **Guard:** `test_expert_grasps_near_arm_a_base` (seeds 1004, 1009) in
  tests/test_task_t0_can_to_metal_bin.py.
- **Coverage:** the corner (x < −0.15, y < −0.08) is ~4 % of the can patch;
  seeds 0–49 had 1 demonstration there, 0–199 have 8 — the reason the
  demonstrations were expanded to 200 (design-decisions D27).

### 33. OpenVINO results differed from PyTorch by up to 2·10⁻²
- **Symptom:** first export of the 2-episode test checkpoints: max |OpenVINO −
  PyTorch| on the same inputs 2.5·10⁻³ (bc_mlp) and 2.2·10⁻² (ACT) —
  larger than expected for 32-bit floats.
- **How found:** compiled the same model with `INFERENCE_PRECISION_HINT` f16
  and f32 and compared each with PyTorch; read the CPU's default property.
- **Cause:** on this Mac (ARM) OpenVINO's CPU plugin runs in **f16** by
  default (`INFERENCE_PRECISION_HINT` = float16). At f32 the difference is
  3·10⁻⁷ (bc_mlp) and 2.5·10⁻⁶ (ACT): the conversion itself is exact.
- **Fix:** `export_openvino.py` reports parity at f32 (conversion check) and
  at the precision it runs at, records that precision, and
  `evaluate_openvino.py` runs at the same one. Whether f16 changes task
  success is for the phase-8 evaluation to show.
- **Guard:** `test_openvino_export_matches_pytorch` (f32, < 10⁻⁴).

### 35. The evaluation script started on a half-trained ACT
- **Symptom:** testing `scripts/evaluate.sh`, the check "refuse a policy with
  no trained checkpoint" was expected to stop on ACT (still training); instead
  a full 50-episode `lerobot-eval` started, competing with ACT's training for
  the GPU.
- **Cause:** LeRobot writes `checkpoints/last/` as soon as the first
  intermediate checkpoint (5k steps) is saved — it points at the newest one —

- **Fix:** stopped it after ≈ 10 min (ACT training continued, step 14k); the
  partial output moved to `outputs/incomplete/` (later removed in the
  outputs clean-up, with the user's approval). `evaluate.sh`
  now also requires LeRobot's "End of training" line in
  `outputs/train/<task>/<policy>.log`.
