As of 2026-10-05, mujoco 3.12.0. **Verified** = run in this repo's environment;
everything else cites its source.

- **Part A — Our scene:** what is in it today, how to run and change it, what

# Part A — Our scene

The task is table waste sorting (see [README](../README.md)): two SO-101 arms
clear a table into pull-out bins and containers.

## A1. What is in the scene

File: [`sim/scenes/dinner_table.xml`](../sim/scenes/dinner_table.xml).
World frame: origin on the floor under the table centre, z up, metres. The
arms sit on the **−y** edge (the front); the overview camera looks from there.

| Part | What | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Table | 0.70 × 0.60 m top, surface at **z = 0.315**; four legs | centred at origin |
| Arm A | SO-101, prefix `left_` | base (−0.23, −0.25, 0.3174) |
| Arm B | SO-101, prefix `right_`, rotated 180° about z | base (+0.23, −0.25, 0.3174) |
| Plastic bin | pull-out drawer under the front edge, blue | x = −0.10 |
| Metal bin | same, grey | x = +0.10 |
| Waste items | **not in the scene file**: the environment attaches the items an episode needs on reset (scene-decisions D17) | placed by the task |
| Cameras | `overview` (aimed at the table), `left_wrist`, `right_wrist` | — |

**Arms.** Model: physicalai's single-arm `so101.xml` (tuned box/sphere gripper
collisions + wrist camera), attached twice. The arms **face each other**: at
zero pan each reaches toward the other, not toward the far side. Base z is
0.3174 because the base mesh extends 2.4 mm below its origin.

**Bins.** Each 0.16 × 0.20 × 0.12 m, built from boxes inside the `table` body,
on a `slide` joint `<name>_bin_slide`: 0 = closed (front panel flush with the
table edge at y = −0.30), 0.18 = fully out toward −y. The handle bar stands
**5 cm** off the front panel. Sites: `<name>_bin_handle` (the bar),
`<name>_bin_hook` (centre of the gap behind the bar — a fingertip here pulls
the bin open), `<name>_bin_interior` (box for "is it inside?" checks).

**Items.** 20 YCB scans in [`sim/assets/objects/ycb/`](../sim/assets/objects/ycb/)
(list in [docs/tasks.md](tasks.md)). On `reset`, `sim/waste_env.py` attaches the
task's items to the scene with MjSpec (`prefix="ycb_"` → body `ycb_<name>`,
joint `ycb_<name>_free`), compiles, and keeps one compiled model per item set.
Mass from the YCB paper (Table II). Collision: cans = exact cylinder;
everything else = the scan's convex hull.

**State layout** (`qpos`): 2 bin slides (plastic, metal) → 12 arm joints
(`left_` then `right_`: pan, lift, elbow, wrist_flex, wrist_roll, gripper) →
7 values (x y z, qw qx qy qz) per attached item. The scene file alone: 14
values; with the T1 can: 21. `ctrl` (12): the arm position-servo targets in
radians, same arm order.

**Keyframes:** `home` (arms at rest, bins closed) and `bins_open` (same, all
bins out), bins and arms only. Generated by `scripts/find_home_pose.py`. In a
model with items attached, a key's item entries are padded with the world
origin — `reset` always places items after loading a key.

**Physics:** `timestep 0.002`, `implicitfast`, elliptic cones, `impratio 10`,
CG solver — physicalai's tested values. Runs at **10.8× real time**.

**Asset files**

| Path | What | License |
|---|---|---|
| [`sim/assets/so101/`](../sim/assets/so101/) | `so101.xml` (unmodified, from [physicalai](https://github.com/openvinotoolkit/physicalai) @ `d58921b`), meshes from [SO-ARM100](https://github.com/TheRobotStudio/SO-ARM100), `SOURCE.md` | Apache 2.0 |
| [`sim/assets/objects/ycb/`](../sim/assets/objects/ycb/) | one folder per item: `model.xml`, scan, 1024² texture, `SOURCE.md` (~40 MB total) | CC BY 4.0 |

## A3. Measured facts

All **verified** in this scene unless noted.

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Servo hold at `home` | 0.0004 rad max error after 3 s; no arm contacts |
| Gripper fingertip gap | ≈ 0.6 cm closed (−0.17 rad), 1.7 cm at 0, 5.6 cm at 0.5, 9.2 cm at 1.0, **≈ 13.5 cm** fully open (1.75 rad) — between jaw collision geoms, approximate |
| Arm reach at grasp height (2–6 cm above the table) | ≈ 0.47 m from the base; far side only to y ≈ 0.18 (table edge 0.30) |
| Reach under the front edge | each arm reaches its own side down to z ≈ 0.10; \|x\| < 0.1 is shared |
| Bin mechanics | stays shut at rest; 0.5 N pull opens fully in ~2 s, 2 N in 0.46 s; stops at 0.18 m |
| Items alone on a floor | all 20 settle; knife rests on its blade edge (15°); round fruit and the cup still creep 1–3 cm/s after 2 s |
| Table space | the 20 items need ≈ 0.49 m² (footprint circles + 8 mm) vs ≈ 0.20 m² the arms can reach → random layouts fit 8–10 → one subset per episode |
| Both arms can touch the tomato can | gripper tip 3.4 / 3.2 cm from its centre = its surface |

**What each arm can do at the bins** (IK in `sim/ik.py`, all contacts
counted, gripper angle left free):

| Motion | Plastic bin (x −0.10) | Metal bin (x +0.10) |
|---|---|---|
| Open it (fingertip into the gap behind the handle, pull 0.18 m) | arm A ✓, arm B ✗ | arm B ✓, arm A ✗ |
| Drop a held can into it (can centre over the open bin) | arm A ✓, arm B ✓ | arm A ✓, arm B ✓ |

Bin positions were chosen with this test (sweep of ±0.09 … ±0.12): ±0.10 is
the closest pair where both openings and all four drops work; at ±0.09 arm B
can no longer open the metal bin.

Gripper tilt (from pointing straight down) for arm A holding the can over the
open metal bin, best of 200 IK restarts: **15°** at ±0.10, **44°** at the
earlier ±0.20. *Correction:* an earlier test reported that at ±0.20 arm A
could only do it upside down (172°). That test kept the solution with the
smallest position error, and an upside-down pose tied with upright ones at
0 mm. The real blocker in the old layout was the 2.5 cm handle.

With the original 2.5 cm handle, no arm could reach into the gap of a closed
bin: the gap was 1 cm in front of the table edge and the gripper hit the
table. Pointing the gripper straight down fails at most bin targets — the arm
is too short that close in front of its own base — so these motions leave the
wrist angle free.

**Grasping the can (arm A).** With the gripper pointing straight down, the
fingertips reach only up to **z ≈ 0.40** — below the can's top (0.417) — and
no tilted approach (15°–75°, any compass direction) reaches a grasp at all. So
the gripper cannot come down onto the can from above. What works: lower the
open, vertical gripper *beside* the can and slide it sideways so the can
passes between the jaws (the jaws are thin plates, open at the sides), close,
then lift with the wrist free to tilt. This works for can positions
x −0.16 … −0.06, y −0.10 … −0.05; closer to the base (x < −0.16) the wrist
camera mount hits the can or the arm hits itself.

**Scripted expert, task T1:** 50/50 seeds succeed. Fixes found by tracing
failures: arm B parks beside its own base instead of returning home (at home
its wrist camera was in arm A's drop path — 4/10 failures); arm A lifts over
the can with the gripper closed and opens only beside it (opening on the way
tipped the can over in 1/40 seeds). The metal bin opens to ≈ 0.16 m of its
0.18 m — enough for the drop.

**Rendering cost:** with three 224×224 cameras in the observation a step takes
≈ 0.15 s (≈ 7× slower than real time); without cameras the environment runs
far faster than real time. Matters for dataset recording.

# Part B — MuJoCo how-to

## B1. Mental model

A scene is one **MJCF** XML model (or an `MjSpec` built in Python) that
compiles to an `MjModel`. Everything hangs off `<worldbody>` as a **kinematic
tree**: a body moves relative to its parent only through the joints declared
inside it; a body with no joint is welded to its parent.
([Modeling guide](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/modeling.html))

| Element | What it is for |
|---|---|
| `<compiler>` | Units and paths: `angle="radian"`, `meshdir`, `autolimits` |
| `<option>` | Physics: `timestep`, `integrator`, `cone`, `impratio`, solver |
| `<default>` | Reusable attribute classes; `childclass` applies one to a whole subtree |
| `<asset>` | Meshes, textures, materials, and whole sub-models (`<model>`) to attach |
| `<worldbody>` | The tree: `body`, `geom`, `joint`, `site`, `camera`, `light`, `frame` |
| `<actuator>` | Motors/servos that `data.ctrl` drives |
| `<keyframe>` | Named full states (`qpos`, `ctrl`) to reset into |

## B2. Scene file and physics options

Keep the robot in its own file and write a separate scene that pulls it in:
the robot file stays untouched, the scene owns the table, objects, lights and
cameras.

```xml
<mujoco model="my_scene">
  <compiler angle="radian" autolimits="true"/>
  <option timestep="0.002" integrator="implicitfast" cone="elliptic" impratio="10"/>
  <asset> … </asset>
  <worldbody> … </worldbody>
  <keyframe> … </keyframe>
</mujoco>
```

- `timestep` is "the single most important parameter affecting the
  speed-accuracy trade-off" ([docs](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/XMLreference.html#option-timestep)).
- `cone="elliptic"`: "a better model of the physical reality" ([docs](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/XMLreference.html#option-cone)).
- `impratio` > 1 makes friction "harder", "reducing slow slippage" — good for
  grasping; not with pyramidal cones ([docs](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/XMLreference.html#option-impratio)).
- `integrator="implicitfast"`: implicit in velocity, drops Coriolis terms;
  stable with stiff servos ([docs](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/XMLreference.html#option-integrator)).

**An attached model's `<option>` is not inherited** (**verified**): MuJoCo
prints `Attach conflict … keeping parent value`. Declare physics in the scene.

## B3. Floor, lights, cameras

```xml
<light pos="0 0 2" dir="0 0 -1" directional="true"/>
<geom name="floor" type="plane" size="0 0 0.05"/>
<camera name="overview" pos="0 -0.75 0.95" mode="targetbody" target="table"/>
```

- A camera's pose is `pos` plus one orientation (`xyaxes`, `euler`, `quat`),
  or `mode="targetbody"` to aim at a body. A camera inside a body moves with
  it — that is a wrist camera.
- Render offscreen: `mujoco.Renderer(model, h, w)` →
  `update_scene(data, camera="overview")` → `render()`.
- **The near clip plane scales with the model** (**verified**): it sits at
  `visual/map/znear` × `stat.extent`. Parked objects grew our extent to 5 m,
  pushing the near plane to 5 cm — past the jaws 3.5 cm in front of each wrist
  camera. `<visual><map znear="0.002"/></visual>` → 1 cm.

## B4. Bring in a robot: `attach`, not `include`

| | `<include file=…>` | `<attach model=… body=… prefix=…>` |
|---|---|---|
| Implemented in | the XML parser (copy-paste) | the model compiler |
| Same file twice? | **No** — "at most once" | Yes, one `prefix` each |
| Name collisions | yours to avoid | `prefix` renames the subtree, its actuators and sensors |

The docs: "Prefer attach to include"
([include](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/XMLreference.html#include),
[attach](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/XMLreference.html#body-attach)).

```xml
<asset>
  <model name="arm" file="path/to/arm.xml"/>
</asset>
<worldbody>
  <frame name="left_mount" pos="-0.23 -0.25 0.315">
    <attach model="arm" body="base" prefix="left_"/>
  </frame>
</worldbody>
```

- `<frame>` is a pure transform — the clean way to place an attached subtree
  ([frame](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/XMLreference.html#body-frame)).
- `body="base"` copies only that subtree; demo objects elsewhere in the
  robot's file are left behind (**verified**).
- Python equivalent, for building scenes procedurally
  ([model editing](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/programming/modeledit.html)):

```python
spec = mujoco.MjSpec()
arm = mujoco.MjSpec.from_file("arm.xml")
spec.worldbody.add_frame(pos=[-0.23, -0.25, 0.315]).attach_body(arm.body("base"), "left_", "")
model = spec.compile()
```

## B5. Fixtures and articulated objects

A body with no joint is welded to the world. Box `size` is **half**-extents.

```xml
<body name="table" pos="0 0 0.3">
  <geom name="table_top" type="box" size="0.35 0.3 0.015"/>   <!-- surface at z = 0.315 -->
</body>
```

A drawer is a body with a `slide` joint (a door: `hinge`): a `range` so it
stops, `damping` so it doesn't fly open, a `site` on the handle as grasp
target. "Is it open?" is then that joint's `qpos`.

```xml
<body name="bin" pos="0 -0.19 -0.085">
  <joint name="bin_slide" type="slide" axis="0 -1 0" range="0 0.18" damping="3"/>
  <geom … />
  <site name="bin_handle" pos="0 -0.135 0.03"/>
</body>
```

Putting it **inside** the table body means it never collides with the table
(parent–child filter, B8).

## B6. Free objects and where their models come from

```xml
<body name="mug" pos="0 0.05 0.36">
  <freejoint name="mug_free"/>
  <geom type="cylinder" size="0.035 0.04" mass="0.15"/>
</body>
```

- `<freejoint>` = 6-DOF joint that does **not** inherit joint defaults
  ([docs](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/XMLreference.html#body-freejoint)).
- It adds 7 `qpos` entries (x y z + quaternion **w x y z**) and 6 `qvel`.
- Spawn slightly above the surface and let it settle.

How projects get object models (checked 2026-10-05):

| Route | Example |
|---|---|
| Scans of real objects | [YCB](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.03143) — download `https://ycb-benchmarks.s3.amazonaws.com/data/google/<id>_google_16k.tgz`, index at `…/data/objects.json`; [Google Scanned Objects](https://github.com/kevinzakka/mujoco_scanned_objects) — 1030 items already in MJCF |
| 3D model library | [RoboCasa](https://github.com/robocasa/robocasa) takes part of its 2,509 objects from Objaverse ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.02523v1)) |
| Text-to-3D | RoboCasa: 1,592 objects from Luma.ai |
| Model it | Blender/CAD — a can is a revolved profile + label |

RoboCasa has 150+ kitchen categories (incl. `can`, `water_bottle`, `yogurt`,
`basket`) but downloads only as packs: Lightwheel 0.79 GB, Objaverse 2.16 GB,
AI-generated 5.77 GB. Converting meshes: [obj2mjcf](https://github.com/kevinzakka/obj2mjcf)
(optionally with CoACD convex decomposition).

**The collision shape matters more than the source.** A convex object can
collide as its own mesh hull (robosuite's `can.xml` does), or better as an
exact primitive with the scan as visual only: exact, cheap, and shape
randomisation is just `size`. Concave objects need a decomposition or
hand-built primitives (B8).

Importing a scan (**verified**): MuJoCo keeps the mesh in its file
coordinates — the YCB can's centre was at (−0.009, 0.084, 0.051) m — so offset
the visual geom by the measured centre. Scan textures are large (4096², 8.9 MB);
1024² is plenty for 640×480 renders.

## B7. Liquids

"Proper simulation of fluid dynamics is beyond the scope of MuJoCo"
([fluid forces](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/computation/fluid.html)) —
its fluid model is drag on rigid bodies. The usual stand-in is many small
spheres. A bowl of spheres tipped over an open bin (**verified**, single core):

| Setting | Result |
|---|---|
| Default solver, 200 × 6 mm spheres | 0.02× real time (562 contacts once they stack) |
| `solver="CG" jacobian="sparse"`, same | 0.46× real time — ~30× faster |
| Bowl moved as a **mocap** body | 55–90 % spilled through the floor–wall seam |
| Bowl as a **free body** pulled by a weld (like a held object) | 60 × 8 mm: 98 % in, 1.9× real time; 120 × 7 mm: 95 %, 0.7×; 200 × 6 mm: 86 %, 0.4× |

Use few, larger spheres, CG + sparse, and a real dynamic container. The
spheres need friction to stay put, so this is a visual/metric proxy, not
water. Fallback: a "filled" flag with no particles. Not yet tested: the SO-101
actually holding and tilting a container.

## B8. Collision vs visual geometry

- Convention: **visual** geoms `contype="0" conaffinity="0" group="2"`;
  **collision** geoms `group="3"` (hidden by default).
- Two geoms are tested for contact only if
  `(contype1 & conaffinity2) || (contype2 & conaffinity1)`; default 1/1 = all collide.
- Geoms in the same body, or a parent and child body, never collide (unless
  the parent is the world)
  ([collision detection](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/computation/index.html#collision-detection)).
- **Mesh geoms collide as their convex hull**
  ([mesh](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/XMLreference.html#asset-mesh)):
  a mug's hollow or a jaw's inner face disappears for contact.

## B9. Keyframes

```xml
<keyframe>
  <key name="home" qpos="…all nq values…" ctrl="…all nu values…"/>
</keyframe>
```

Reset with `mujoco.mj_resetDataKeyframe(model, data, key_id)`. Set **both**
`qpos` and `ctrl` — with position servos, `ctrl` 0 drags the arm to zero
([docs](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/XMLreference.html#keyframe)).
Print `[m.joint(j).name for j in range(m.njnt)]` for the `qpos` order rather
than guessing.

Two traps (**verified**):

- **A new joint can shift every key silently.** Joints are ordered by where
  their body sits in `<worldbody>`; our bins (inside the table, before the
  arms) went to the *front* of `qpos`.
- **A short key does not put free objects at their spawn pose.** Missing
  free-joint entries became position (0, 0, 0) — the can landed on the floor
  at the origin. List every free object in every key; generate keys rather
  than typing them. (A key that is too *long* is a load error instead:
  `invalid qpos size, expected 154, got 155`.)

## B10. Check a scene

```python
m = mujoco.MjModel.from_xml_path("scene.xml")
d = mujoco.MjData(m)
mujoco.mj_resetDataKeyframe(m, d, 0)
for _ in range(1000):
    mujoco.mj_step(m, d)
```

| Check | How | Pass |
|---|---|---|
| Compiles | `MjModel.from_xml_path` | no error; read every `WARNING` |
| Names | list joints, actuators, cameras | prefixed, unique, expected counts |
| Stable at rest | 1000+ steps from a key | `np.isfinite(d.qpos).all()`, objects settle |
| Servos hold | `abs(qpos[arm] - ctrl).max()` | ≈ 0 |
| Contacts | `d.contact[:d.ncon]` → body names | only intended pairs |
| Heights | body `xpos` z | surface + half-height |
| Cameras | render each and look | subject in frame, nothing clipped |
| Masses | `m.body_mass` | no zeros or absurd values |
| Speed | time 1000 steps | above real time |
